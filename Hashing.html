What is hashing, really?

Imagine a wall of numbered lockers. If you want to find "locker for student #47" instantly — no walking down the row checking labels — you need a rule that turns "47" straight into a locker number. That rule is a hash function. The number it produces is the index (or address), and the wall of lockers is the hash table. Instead of searching, you calculate.

Every method on this page answers the same question in a different way: given a key, what quick bit of arithmetic gets me to an index? Some just reuse the key. Others chop it up, square it, or fold it in half.

WHY BOTHER

Looking something up by calculating its address is O(1) — constant time — versus scanning a list, which is O(n).

THE CATCH

Two different keys can land on the same address. That's called a collision — every method below has to live with the possibility.

A COLLISION, IN MINIATURE

Keys 12 and 22 both hash to table[2] under "key mod 10." Real hash tables handle this with chaining or probing — a topic for another day. For now, just notice: it happens to every method below.
